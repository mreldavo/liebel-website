Invest in AI, but don't invest in AI

WHY TO BUY

Demand for AI is still bigger than the supply. The five biggest spenders (Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta and Oracle) plan to put about $720 billion into AI infrastructure this year, far more than last year, and Amazon and Microsoft both say they still can't build capacity fast enough.1,2 People are adopting AI faster than they took up the PC or the internet, and US productivity has run above its long-term trend for two straight quarters.2

Stocks are expensive, but profits are growing fast enough to justify much of the price. The S&P 500 trades at about 19 times expected earnings: above its long-run average, but nowhere near the dot-com peak.3 On this view, every bubble warning so far has been followed by new highs, and the recent selloff is a chance to buy.

WHY TO SELL

It's the financing that's fragile. The biggest tech companies are borrowing heavily to build data centers, their free cash flow is shrinking, and they're increasingly each other's customers, suppliers and lenders, so trouble at one can spread to the rest. The IMF, Ray Dalio and Michael Burry are all warning about this right now.4,5 Burry has bought put options on chip stocks and on Nebius that pay off if they fall before late 2027.6

Joachim Klement of Panmure Liberum estimates the big spenders would need to add $2–5 trillion in new revenue every year to justify what they're spending, and he puts the S&P 500 at 5,000 by the end of 2027, more than 35% below today.7 The cracks are already showing in heavily indebted players like CoreWeave, whose stock and bonds have been swinging hard. Demand can be completely real and investors can still lose a lot if the debt behind it can't be paid back.

The two arguments don't really cancel out. The buy case is about demand and the sell case is about debt, so both can be right at the same time.
